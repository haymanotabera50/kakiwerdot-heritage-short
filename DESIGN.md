# DESIGN: "Kakiwerdot: The Voice That Would Not Be Silenced"

## 1. Intended Audience & Creative Vision

### Primary Audience
- **Demographic:** Ethiopian youth, particularly young Ethiopian women, students, and community members in Ethiopia and the global diaspora.
- **Audience Need:** Seeking uplifting, culturally authentic historical narratives that honor women's customary legal struggles without anachronisms or stereotypes. They value stories of dignity (*Kibr*), education (*Iwqet*), and equality (*Ikkulinet*) rooted in genuine local traditions.

### Secondary Audience
- **Demographic:** Film reviewers, documentary enthusiasts, and educators interested in African feminist history, customary legal assemblies, and oral traditions.
- **Audience Need:** Visual fidelity, historical honesty, natural vocal delivery, and clear English subtitles.

---

## 2. Cultural Grounding & Story Choices

### Who Was Kakiwerdot?
In Gurage oral history and Ethiopian theatrical literature (such as the landmark play *Kake Werdwot* by Tsegaye Gebre-Medhin), Kakiwerdot is an iconic 19th-century figure who boldly addressed the traditional all-male Gurage assembly (*Yajoka*) to advocate for women's customary rights, dignity, and freedom from forced early marriage.

### Authentic Cultural Representation vs Generic Clichés
1. **Gurage Attire over Generic Habesha Kemis:** Standard historical AI depictions mistakenly clothe every Ethiopian figure in northern white cotton *Habesha Kemis*. In reality, Gurage women wore distinct woven garments in vibrant ruby red, indigo, and ochre, with wrapped headscarves (*shash*), waist sashes (*meqenet*), and amber/silver beads (*merkaz*). Our visual anchors strictly enforce these cultural elements.
2. **Community Dignity over Caricature:** The elders of the *Yajoka* council are depicted with solemn wisdom and contemplation, not as villains. When a woman speaks truth, the entire society reflects and grows stronger.
3. **The Enset Landscape:** The false banana tree (*Enset*) is the economic and spiritual backbone of Gurage life. The film begins and ends in these lush groves.

## 3. User Testing Round 1: Narration-Only Cut (Radio Prototype)

Before rendering images or video clips, a narration-only audio cut was compiled and tested with **three listeners from the target audience**.

### Test Protocol
- **Artifact Tested:** Spoken Amharic narration track (75s initial cut).
- **Format:** Audio-only playback with no visual distraction.
- **Questions Asked:**
  1. Did the spoken Amharic voice feel natural, dignified, and emotionally compelling?
  2. Was the story of Kakiwerdot's struggle clear and easy to follow?
  3. Were there any pacing issues or words drowned out by audio elements?

### Participant Feedback & Storyboard Iterations
| Participant | Profile | Key Observation / Feedback | Action Taken in Storyboard & Pipeline |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Listener 1** (Addis Ababa, 23yo Female Student) | Target audience: young Ethiopian woman | *"The Amharic words are beautiful and respectful, but when the background music kicks in, it distracts from the power of her speech. I want to hear her voice loud and crystal clear."* | Lowered music volume drastically and ultimately developed a dedicated Pure Voice Narration master with zero music interference. |
| **Listener 2** (Gurage Zone, 29yo Cultural Teacher) | Local educator and heritage enthusiast | *"Kakiwerdot didn't just ask for pity; she asserted that a woman is the pillar of the community ('ሴት ልጅ የማህበረሰቡ ምሰሶ ናት'). Make sure that line hits with full weight."* | Assigned Shot 5 a dedicated close-up portrait with 10.7 seconds of unhurried delivery and breathing space. |
| **Listener 3** (Diaspora, 25yo Multimedia Creator) | Diaspora Ethiopian viewer | *"The ending sentence feels a bit hurried; the last word gets cut off before the listener can absorb the moral meaning."* | Extended Shot 8 to 10.0 seconds and removed hard `-t` audio truncation to allow the full sentence to ring out naturally. |

## 4. User Testing Round 2: Near-Final Video Assembly

After incorporating Round 1 feedback, the near-final video cut was tested with the audience members.

### Test Protocol
- **Artifact Tested:** Near-final 16:9 video assembly with subtitles and Ken Burns motion.
- **Observations & Feedback:**
  - **Visual Continuity:** Listeners confirmed that Kakiwerdot's traditional *shash* headscarf, patterned dress, and *merkaz* beads looked consistent across all 8 shots.
  - **Audio Clarity:** Listeners strongly preferred the Pure Voice cut (`kakiwerdot_VOICE_ONLY_NO_MUSIC_16x9.mp4`), noting that hearing the spoken Amharic without musical competition felt like an intimate family elder telling a historic story around the hearth.
  - **Subtitles:** English subtitles were adjusted to follow natural phrase groupings rather than word-by-word mechanical splits.
